## Grade 1

50 questions across Monday-Friday, 10 per day. Focus: counting, comparing numbers, addition and subtraction within 20, unknowns, and simple story problems.

1. Count by ones from 1 to 20.
2. What number comes after 7?
3. What number comes before 12?
4. Write the number that has 5 ones.
5. Write the number that has 1 ten and 4 ones.
6. Compare using `<`, `>`, or `=`: 8 ___ 6.
7. Compare using `<`, `>`, or `=`: 13 ___ 13.
8. How many objects are in a group of 9 stars?
9. What number is 1 more than 10?
10. What number is 1 less than 15?
11. Add: 3 + 2.
12. Add: 5 + 4.
13. Add: 7 + 1.
14. Add: 6 + 3.
15. Add: 8 + 2.
16. Complete: 4 + ___ = 9.
17. Complete: ___ + 3 = 8.
18. Complete: 10 = 6 + ___.
19. Mia has 4 crayons. Her teacher gives her 3 more. How many crayons does Mia have now?
20. There are 5 birds in a tree. 2 more birds arrive. How many birds are in the tree now?
21. Subtract: 7 - 2.
22. Subtract: 9 - 4.
23. Subtract: 10 - 3.
24. Subtract: 8 - 5.
25. Subtract: 6 - 1.
26. Complete: 9 - ___ = 5.
27. Complete: ___ - 2 = 6.
28. Complete: 10 - 7 = ___.
29. Leo has 8 blocks. He gives 3 blocks to his sister. How many blocks does Leo have left?
30. There are 10 apples. A child eats 2 apples. How many apples remain?
31. Count by twos: 2, 4, 6, ___, ___.
32. Count by fives: 5, 10, ___, 20.
33. Which number is greater: 14 or 9?
34. Which number is less: 7 or 11?
35. Draw or describe a group that has 6 objects.
36. A bus has 6 children. Then 4 more children get on. How many children are on the bus?
37. A box has 9 balls. Two balls roll away. How many balls are left?
38. Sam has 5 red stickers and 5 blue stickers. How many stickers does Sam have?
39. There are 7 ducks in a pond. There are 3 fewer fish than ducks. How many fish are there?
40. Nora has 10 beads. She uses 4 beads. How many beads does she have left?
41. Find the missing number: 7 + ___ = 10.
42. Find the missing number: ___ + 4 = 11.
43. Find the missing number: 12 - ___ = 8.
44. Find the missing number: ___ - 3 = 6.
45. Ben has 6 toy cars. Ava has 2 more toy cars than Ben. How many toy cars does Ava have?
46. There are 12 students in a room. Four students leave. How many students remain?
47. Which equation matches this story: A child has 3 apples and gets 5 more?
48. Explain how you know that 9 is greater than 6.
49. Make a number sentence that equals 10.
50. Write or draw a story for the equation 5 + 4 = 9.

## Grade 4

50 questions across Monday-Friday, 10 per day. Focus: place value, multi-digit operations, multiplicative comparison, and a strong presence of data and graphs. Questions 11-30 and 41-45 use tables, picture graphs, bar graphs, and line plots as specified in the Gate 2 review.

1-10. Place-value, rounding, addition, subtraction, estimation, and multi-step quantity questions as presented in the approved Grade 4 Gate 2 set.

11-15. Use the favorite-fruit table: Apples 8, Bananas 5, Oranges 7, Grapes 10. Answer which is most, which is fewest, apples plus oranges, the difference between grapes and bananas, and the total surveyed.

16-20. Use the picture graph where one star represents 2 books: Ana 4 stars, Ben 3, Carlos 5, Dana 2. Answer each student's books, differences, totals, and who read 6 books.

21-25. Use the visitor table: Monday 24, Tuesday 31, Wednesday 18, Thursday 31, Friday 26. Identify the fewest, equal days, requested sums/differences, and total.

26-30. Use bar-graph data: walking 12, bus 9, car 6, bicycle 3; then use line-plot data 2, 2, 3, 3, 3, 4, 5. Answer least, differences, total, mode, and range.

31-40. Compare greatest/least pages, combine selected page counts, interpret a pictograph with 4 plants per square, solve collection, multiplication, division, and place-value applications as presented in the approved Grade 4 Gate 2 set.

41-50. Solve the survey, bar-graph, line-plot, multiplication, subtraction, explanation, and data-creation questions as presented in the approved Grade 4 Gate 2 set. Questions 48 and 50 are open-response reasoning items.

## Grade 5

11-20. Ordered pairs, coordinates, relative position, plotting E(1,6) and F(5,2), horizontal/vertical moves, and writing an ordered pair from coordinates.

21-30. Coordinate distances, axes, rectangle dimensions, perimeter, horizontal/vertical distance, and a robot path.

31-40. Coordinate-map applications, garden area/perimeter, point translations, square dimensions/perimeter, path length, multiplication, and fraction scaling.

41-50. Rectangle area/perimeter, multi-step coordinate movement, explanation of axes and ordered pairs, identifying a square, scale conversion, and the corrected Question 49 above. Question 50 asks how coordinate order changes point location.
